Group A: Short Questions (2 Marks)

1. Define the term “Sitemap” in the planning phase.

A sitemap is a structured outline or diagram that shows all the pages of a website and their relationships, helping in planning navigation and content organization.

2. What is the purpose of the “Launch” phase in web development?

The Launch phase involves making the website live on the Internet after final testing, configuration, and deployment so users can access it.

3. Why is “Documentation” important in the development lifecycle?

Documentation helps developers understand, maintain, update, and troubleshoot the website in the future, especially when team members change.

4. Differentiate between Front-end and Back-end development.
Front-end Development	Back-end Development
User interface	        Server-side logic
Visible to users	    Hidden from users
Uses HTML, CSS, JS	    Uses PHP, Python, DB
Focuses on design	    Focuses on data

Group B: Long Questions (4 Marks)

5. “Planning is the most critical phase of web development.” Discuss.

Planning is the foundation of a successful website. Mistakes at this stage affect the entire project.

Key activities in the Planning phase:

Defining website goals and objectives

Identifying target audience

Creating sitemap and navigation structure

Deciding technologies and resources

Setting budget and timeline

Conclusion: Proper planning reduces errors, saves time, and ensures client satisfaction.

6. Explain the iterative nature of the Maintenance phase. Why is a website never truly “finished”?

The Maintenance phase is continuous and repetitive (iterative).

Reasons:

Content needs regular updates

Bugs and security issues must be fixed

Technology and browsers evolve

User requirements change

Conclusion: Since websites must adapt to changes, they are never truly “finished.”

7. Describe the process of “Information Gathering.” Why is understanding the target audience crucial?

Information Gathering is the first step where requirements are collected from clients and stakeholders.

Activities include:

Client interviews

Requirement analysis

Market research

Understanding goals and competitors

Importance of target audience:

Helps design user-friendly interfaces

Ensures relevant content

Improves user engagement and satisfaction

Group C: Scenario-Based Questions (5 Marks)

8. Persuasive argument against skipping the Testing phase

Skipping the Testing phase is risky and may cost more in the long run.

Potential Risks:

1. Security Risks

Vulnerabilities may allow hacking

Customer data may be exposed

2. Functionality Issues

Broken links

Payment failures

Form submission errors

3. Compatibility Problems

Website may not work on all browsers/devices

Conclusion: Testing ensures reliability, security, and user trust. Skipping it may damage reputation and revenue.

9. Developer started coding without design or sitemap – Analysis

What went wrong:

No proper planning or design phase

No sitemap or wireframe

Client expectations were not documented

Consequences:

Layout mismatch

Time and effort wasted

Client dissatisfaction

Correct approach:

Conduct planning and information gathering

Create sitemap and design prototype

Get client approval before coding

10. Outdated exam schedules – Neglected phase

The Maintenance phase was neglected.

Activities involved in Maintenance phase:

Updating content (exam schedules, notices)

Fixing errors and broken links

Improving performance and security

Regular monitoring

Conclusion: Regular maintenance keeps the website useful, reliable, and engaging for users.
